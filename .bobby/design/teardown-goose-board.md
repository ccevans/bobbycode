## What's good — the *thinking*
The oldest interface for "a thing advancing through ordered stages toward a goal." The board is a
**spiral of numbered cells** winding inward; every player is a **token whose position on the track is
its entire state** — no bar, no percent, just *how far along the spiral you are*. The **centre is the
goal/gate**: reaching it is winning. Some cells are special (hazards / shortcuts) — the ancestor of
"WAITING ON YOU." It is the purest argument that **position on an ordered path is a complete,
instantly-legible status**, and that a race-to-a-goal is a warmer, more human frame than a pipeline
graph. A single glance answers "who's ahead, who's stuck, how close to done."

## What we take
**Structure only:** an ordered numbered track where **position = stage**, and a **distinguished final
cell = the gate/done**. It reinforces the transit-line direction (a route with a terminus) and offers a
playful "race to done" voice if we want the child tickets to read as tokens advancing toward the merge.

## Nostalgia check (required)
This is an **antique parlour game**. The nostalgia signal is "Victorian race-to-the-finish / board-game
whimsy." That could be a *charming* message ("your feature is racing to done") — or a *toy* message that
undercuts a serious dev tool. **We take zero of the ornate/antique surface.** The B&W engraving look, the
spiral, the flourishes are all left behind; only the abstract "ordered track + goal cell" survives, rendered
in our own clean light palette. Flagged so the choice is deliberate, not nostalgic-by-reflex.
